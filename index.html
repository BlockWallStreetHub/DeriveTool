Part 1

The sneaker deal

Before any crypto, here is the whole idea with a pair of sneakers.

A store sells a hot sneaker for $100. You think the price will jump soon. The store says: “Pay me $10 today and I promise you can buy it for $100 any time before the deadline, even if the price goes up.”

That $10 is not refundable. That promise is an option. Drag the slider to see what the sneaker costs at the deadline.

The big lesson: the most you can ever lose is the $10 deposit. But you do not make money until the sneaker passes $110 ($100 locked-in price + $10 deposit). Keep that “locked-in price plus deposit” idea. It shows up everywhere below.
Part 2

Six words to know

Every option uses the same few words. Here they are in plain English next to the sneaker deal.

Spot price

The price of ETH right now.

Sneaker: what the store charges today.

Strike price

The locked-in price written in your deal.

Sneaker: the $100.

Premium

What you pay up front for the deal. If it goes badly, this is the most you can lose.

Sneaker: the $10 deposit.

Expiry

The deadline. On Derive, options end at 8:00 a.m. UTC. In December that is 2:00 a.m. Chicago time.

Sneaker: the last day of the deal.

Call

A deal to buy at the strike. You want the price to go up.

Sneaker: the deal above.

Put

A deal to sell at the strike. You want the price to go down.

Sneaker: a deal to sell yours for $100 even if the price crashes.

The one idea that explains everything

Premium = Real value + Hope value
Real value (the pros say “intrinsic value”): how much the deal is worth if you used it right now. For a call: ETH price minus strike, but never less than $0.
Hope value (the pros say “time value”): the extra you pay for the chance that things get better before the deadline. It melts away as the deadline gets close.

Your real example, step by step

  • ETH costs $2,487.30. You look at a call with a $2,500 strike that ends Dec 25.
  • The seller asks $229.70 for it. That is the premium.
  • $2,487.30 is $12.70 below the $2,500 locked-in price. Using the deal today would be silly, so the real value is $0. All $229.70 is hope value. We call this out of the money (traders also say “at the money” because it is so close).
  • To make a profit on Dec 25, ETH must finish above $2,500 + $229.70 = $2,729.70. That is about 9.7% higher than today.
  • The most you can lose is the $229.70 you paid. If you only buy 0.1 of a contract, that is $22.97.
  • Compare the $2,300 call: it costs $342.70. ETH is already $187.30 above $2,300, so that is real value, and the other $155.40 is hope value. It needs ETH above $2,642.70 (about 6.2% higher). It costs more but needs a smaller jump.

Part 8

Cheat sheet

The formulas

Call real value = ETH price − strike (never below $0)
Put real value = strike − ETH price (never below $0)
Hope value = premium − real value
Call profit starts at = strike + premium
Put profit starts at = strike − premium
Most you can lose = premium × how many you bought

Mistakes to avoid

  • “Buying less lowers my profit price.” No. The profit price is a price level. Buying 0.1 instead of 1 only changes the dollars won or lost.
  • “Time helps me.” No. As a buyer, time only melts hope value. Price moves and bigger expected swings are what raise the price.
  • “In the money means profit.” Not always. You also paid a premium, so you may be in the money and still down.
  • Wrong date. Always check the expiry on the order ticket. A $2,500 call expiring in 3 days is a very different deal than one expiring Dec 25.

Greeks in one line each

  • Delta: how many dollars the option moves when ETH moves $1.
  • Theta: how many dollars it melts per day.
  • Vega: how many dollars it gains if expected swings rise by one point.

How Derive works

  • Options are European-style: the deal is settled on the deadline, but you can sell any time on the order book.
  • They end at 8:00 a.m. UTC and settle to an average ETH price over the last 30 minutes, paid in USDC.
  • Buying an option risks only the premium. Selling an option can lose much more and needs margin (collateral). Beginners should stick to buying.
  • Derive has said it is not available to US customers. Check the rules where you live before connecting a wallet.
